ReciMe alternative for iPhone: ReciPics vs ReciMe

Short answer: ReciPics may suit you if you cook from an iPhone with iOS 26 or later and want recipes from photos of ingredients, allergy and diet profiles, and a grocery list sorted by store section.

ReciMe may suit you if you also cook from an iPad, Mac, Android phone or the web, want cloud sync, or save recipes from YouTube, Pinterest or Facebook.

Comparing recipe apps? ReciPics (App Store name: “ReciPics: Save My Recipes”) and ReciMe: Recipes & Meal Planner both save recipes from links into a cookbook on your phone. ReciPics is for iPhone with iOS 26 or later; the App Store does not list it for iPad, Mac or other platforms. The table puts the public facts side by side, so you can choose what suits you.

Where ReciMe may suit you better

  • You use more than an iPhone. ReciMe lists iPad, Mac with Apple silicon, Apple Vision and Android, plus a web app and a Chrome extension. ReciPics is listed for iPhone.
  • Your iPhone runs an older iOS. ReciMe needs iOS 17 or later; ReciPics needs iOS 26 or later.
  • You save recipes from YouTube, Pinterest or Facebook. ReciMe names these sources; ReciPics does not.
  • You want your recipes synced across devices. ReciMe states cloud sync; ReciPics has no sync.
  • You want a metric/imperial converter inside the app (ReciMe Plus). ReciPics offers a converter on its website.
  • You prefer a published free limit. ReciMe states 5 imports every 7 days and lists grocery lists, meal plan and cookbooks as free. ReciPics does not publish numbers.
  • You want to order groceries from the app.
  • You want a longer trial: 7 days, compared with 3 days on the ReciPics yearly plan.
  • You want the app in French, Portuguese or Spanish. ReciMe lists five App Store languages, ReciPics two.

Where ReciPics fits

  • You want recipes from what is in front of you: take a photo of ingredients to get recipe suggestions, scan a cooked dish to identify the recipe, or speak ingredients or meal ideas.
  • You save recipes from links: paste a recipe link, or share directly from your browser or social apps. ReciPics reads the page, caption or video description. This does not work for every post, and results vary by site and post: some sites block the import, and recipes taken from a video need their amounts checked. See Save recipes from Instagram and TikTok.
  • You cook for people with allergies or special diets: create profiles with allergies, dietary preferences and health considerations, and ReciPics uses them when it creates recipes. It can make mistakes, so always check ingredients yourself if you have allergies.
  • You like to change recipes: edit ingredient names, amounts, units and steps, or ask the in-recipe AI assistant by text or voice to swap an ingredient or adapt the recipe for a diet. It applies the change to your recipe.
  • You shop from a list: the grocery list is organized into store sections, and you can share it with family or roommates in one tap, without an account.

ReciPics is free to download and includes a limited free allowance of AI recipes. ReciPics Premium is weekly or yearly; prices for your region are shown in the app and in the App Store.

Questions

Is ReciPics an alternative to ReciMe?
For some people. Both apps save recipes from links into a cookbook. ReciPics runs on iPhone with iOS 26 or later, suggests recipes from photos of ingredients, identifies recipes from photos of dishes and has allergy and diet profiles. ReciMe also runs on iPad, Mac, Android and the web and names more import sources.
Which devices does ReciPics run on?
iPhone with iOS 26 or later. iPad, Mac and Apple Vision are not listed on the App Store, and there is no Android app. ReciMe lists iPhone (iOS 17 or later), iPad, Mac with Apple M1 or later, Apple Vision and Android.
What does ReciPics cost compared with ReciMe?
Both are free to download. ReciPics Premium is weekly or yearly; on the US App Store the yearly plan is $29.99 and the weekly plan $4.99, with a 3-day free trial on the yearly plan for eligible new subscribers. ReciMe states $39.99 per year in the US for ReciMe Plus and a 7-day free trial. Checked 16 November 2026; prices may have changed.
