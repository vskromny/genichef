        How it works

        1. Copy the link of a recipe page from your browser and paste it above.
        2. Our server fetches that one page and reads only the structured recipe data the website publishes for machines (schema.org Recipe). We do not use an AI model for this.
        3. You see the title, ingredients, steps, times and servings as published, with a link to the source at the top and the bottom.

        The result has no web address of its own. It stays in this browser tab until you close or reload it. The page sets no cookies.

        Questions

        Which recipe websites work?

        Websites that publish structured recipe data (schema.org Recipe) in their pages. Many recipe websites and food blogs add this data so that search engines can show their recipes, but not every site does. If a page has no such data, the tool tells you and shows nothing.

        Why does a link say it is not supported?

        Usually the page does not publish structured recipe data, or the page could not be reached. Some websites also ask not to be read by tools like this one, and we respect that. We don't use other methods to read a page. Links from social networks and video platforms are not supported.

        Is the recipe saved anywhere?

        No. The result exists only in this browser tab and is gone when you close or reload it. We don't store the link or the recipe. To keep a recipe, open the source or save it in the ReciPics app.

        Is there a limit?

        Yes. The tool is free, without an account, and allows a limited number of lookups per day. If you reach the limit, try again tomorrow.

        Who owns the recipe?

        The recipe belongs to its author and the website that published it. Each result links to the source, where you find the full article, photos and tips. Website owners and authors can ask to exclude a site at hello@recipics.app.
