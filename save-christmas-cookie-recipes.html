One cookbook for the baking season

Before December gets busy, get the family recipes off the cards and into one cookbook on your iPhone. Keep the cards: they are the original, and you can compare the recipe with them. The recipes are with you in the kitchen and at the store.

How it works

  1. In ReciPics, tap “Take a photo” and photograph the card or cookbook page, or choose a photo of it from your library.
  2. ReciPics reads the text on the photo and shows the recipe it found. Tap it to open it.
  3. Check the ingredients, amounts and steps, then tap “Add to Cookbook”.

Tips for photographing old cards

  • Use good light and avoid shadows on the card.
  • Lay the card flat.
  • Get the whole card in the frame.
  • After the scan, compare the amounts, oven temperature and baking time with the card.

What to expect

ReciPics reads recipes from photos with AI, and AI can make mistakes. After a scan, the app shows the hint “Please check amounts — handwriting can be misread”. Compare the recipe with the card before you bake, especially amounts and temperatures. ReciPics may add short step titles and suggested ingredient alternatives (shown as “alternative”). These are not from the original.

Clear, flat, well-lit photos work best. Very faint, smudged or tightly written cards are harder to read.

Check ingredients yourself for allergies or intolerances; ReciPics can misread or miss an ingredient.

Bake from it

  • Edit ingredients and steps, for example when a word was misread or you changed the recipe.
  • Scale servings (1x, 2x, 4x), for example for a double batch.
  • Add the ingredients to your grocery list, tick items off while you shop, and share the list as text or PDF.
  • Tick off ingredients while you bake and have the steps read aloud.
  • Share a recipe as text or PDF, for example your own family recipes.
  • Find it again in the Cookbook tab, or search by recipe name or ingredient.

Each photo scan uses the app's AI allowance, which is limited without Premium.
