        One cookbook for the Christmas menu

        Christmas cooking means recipes from everywhere: the roast your family asks for every year, a Stollen you want to try, three kinds of cookies, a pot of mulled wine for the neighbours. Save them as you find them in November. In December they are all in the Cookbook tab, not scattered across bookmarks, screenshots and open tabs.

        How to save a recipe from a link

        1. Open the recipe page in your browser and copy the link. On Instagram or TikTok, tap Share and choose “Copy link”.
        2. In ReciPics, tap “Paste a link”, paste it and tap “Import”.
        3. ReciPics reads the page with AI and saves the title, photo, ingredients and steps to your cookbook. It usually takes about half a minute, sometimes longer, depending on the page.

        For Instagram and TikTok, the app reads the recipe from the caption and, if the caption has none, tries the video. Such recipes are marked “Extracted from the video — check quantities”.

        Which sites work?

        Many recipe websites and food blogs, especially those that show the recipe without a login. Results vary by site. Some sites don't allow importing; then the app tells you. On Instagram and TikTok, public posts work best; private accounts can't be imported.

        Before the cooking starts

        • Edit ingredients and steps, for example to note how you make the roast.
        • Scale servings (1x, 2x, 4x), for example when more guests come.
        • Add the ingredients to your grocery list, tick items off while you shop, and share the list as text or PDF.
        • Share a recipe as text or PDF, so everyone bakes the same Stollen.
        • Tick off ingredients while you cook and have the steps read aloud.
        • Find it again in the Cookbook tab, or search by recipe name or ingredient.

        Always check ingredients yourself for allergies or intolerances.

        Questions

        Can I save Christmas recipes from Instagram or TikTok too?

        Yes, in the app. Copy the link of the post or video and paste it into ReciPics. The app reads the recipe from the caption and, if there is none, tries the video; such recipes are marked “Extracted from the video — check quantities”. The web tool on this page reads recipe websites only.

        What is left of a page full of ads and a long story?

        The recipe. The web tool shows the recipe data the site publishes, with a link to the source at the top and the bottom. The app saves the title, photo, ingredients and steps to your cookbook.

        How does the web tool work?

        Our server fetches that one page and reads only the structured recipe data the website publishes for machines (schema.org Recipe). We do not use an AI model for this. The result has no web address of its own and stays in this browser tab until you close or reload it. The page sets no cookies.

        Is the recipe saved anywhere?

        No. The result exists only in this browser tab and is gone when you close or reload it. We don't store the link or the recipe. To keep a recipe, open the source or save it in the ReciPics app.

        Is there a limit?

        Yes. The tool is free, without an account, and allows a limited number of lookups per day. If you reach the limit, try again tomorrow.

        Who owns the recipe?

        The recipe belongs to its author and the website that published it. Each result links to the source, where you find the full article, photos and tips. Website owners and authors can ask to exclude a site at hello@recipics.app.
