One cookbook for the Christmas menu

The Christmas menu is spread across a dozen tabs: the roast from one food blog, the Stollen from a recipe site, cookies from a reel, the mulled wine from a page you can't find again. Save each recipe as soon as you find it. By December the whole menu is in one cookbook on your iPhone, ready when the family asks what's for dinner.

Collect the menu course by course

  • Starters: the soup or salad you spotted on a food blog.
  • The roast: goose, turkey, beef or a vegetarian main, with the oven steps.
  • Sides: red cabbage, dumplings, potatoes, gravy.
  • Baking: Stollen and Christmas cookies from recipe sites or a reel.
  • Drinks: mulled wine for the neighbours.

Every recipe you import lands in the Cookbook tab. Search by recipe name or ingredient to find it again.

How to save a recipe from a link

  1. Open the recipe page in your browser and copy the link. On Instagram or TikTok, tap Share and choose “Copy link”.
  2. In ReciPics, tap “Paste a link”, paste it and tap “Import”.
  3. ReciPics reads the page with AI and saves the title, photo, ingredients and steps to your cookbook. It usually takes about half a minute, sometimes longer, depending on the page.

For Instagram and TikTok, the app reads the recipe from the caption and, if the caption has none, tries the video. Such recipes are marked “Extracted from the video — check quantities”.

Which sites work?

Many recipe websites and food blogs, especially those that show the recipe without a login. Results vary by site. Not every site works: some sites don't allow importing. Then the app tells you, and you can snap a photo of the recipe or paste it as text instead. On Instagram and TikTok, public posts work best; private accounts can't be imported.

Cooking for the whole family

  • Scale servings (1x, 2x, 4x) when more guests come.
  • Add each recipe's ingredients to your grocery list, where they are grouped by recipe. Tick items off while you shop and share the list as text or PDF.
  • Share a recipe as text or PDF, so everyone bakes the same Stollen.
  • Edit ingredients and steps, for example to note how your family makes the roast.
  • Tick off ingredients while you cook and have the steps read aloud.

Always check ingredients yourself for allergies or intolerances.

Questions

When should I start saving Christmas recipes?

Whenever you come across them. If you save recipes in November as you find them, the whole menu is in one cookbook by December, and you can add the ingredients to your grocery list before the big shop.

Can I save Christmas recipes from Instagram or TikTok too?

Yes. Copy the link of the post or video and paste it into ReciPics. The app reads the recipe from the caption and, if there is none, tries the video; such recipes are marked “Extracted from the video — check quantities”.

How does the family cook from the same recipes?

Share a recipe as text or PDF, for example the Stollen or the roast, so everyone works from the same version. Family recipes on old handwritten cards can be photographed: see Save your family's Christmas cookie recipes.
